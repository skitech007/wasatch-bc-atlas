Forecasts before the nightly job started were rebuilt afterwards: the same forecast code, run as of each past morning on what each model actually forecast then (Open-Meteo's archive of past model runs). Three differences from a live forecast: the storm direction comes from what the 700 mb wind actually did rather than what was forecast, which flatters the reconstruction slightly; the snow ratio uses only the 9,895 ft temperature; and there's no wind. Anything marked ↺ is reconstructed; everything else was saved live that morning.

Water: average miss by how far ahead · inches of melted snow per day, vs the Atwater gauge

Each morning the worker saves the forecast for the next 7 days. Once a day has happened, each saved forecast for it is compared with what the Atwater SNOTEL (8,750 ft, in Alta) measured. "Alta blend" is this site's corrected forecast; the model columns are each model as other apps show it, uncorrected. Lower is better; the best in each row is highlighted.

Temperature: average miss · °F at Atwater

Daily high and low, corrected blend vs the same blend without the temperature correction. "Measured" is the warmest and coldest of Atwater's on-the-hour readings, the same sampling as the hourly forecast; SNOTEL's own daily high, which catches brief peaks between hours, runs about 1°F warmer.

Scored the PEAKS way · the tests OpenSnow used for PEAKS 2.0

OpenSnow's PEAKS 2.0 announcement (Oct 2026) scored models three ways: average miss (the tables above), the share of "significant errors", and how often each model was the best. Significant error here, as theirs: water more than 0.5" off, a miss (0.1"+ fell, under 0.02" forecast) or a false alarm (0.1"+ forecast, under 0.02" fell); temperature more than 3°F off on the high or low.
