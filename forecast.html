How these numbers are made

  1. Water. Hourly precipitation from four weather models for Alta's forecast point (40.5884, −111.6386, 9,895 ft), via Open-Meteo.
  2. Alta correction. The models smooth the Wasatch into blobs and miss how much falls at Alta. For the winters of (Nov 15 – Apr 15) we compared what each model forecast 1 to 7 days ahead with what fell at the Atwater SNOTEL, and multiply each model's water by its factor for that lead day (days 8–15 use day 7):
    Model1 day out234567
    GFS jumps at day 5 and GEM at day 3, where each model switches to a coarser grid. Models with smaller day-to-day error after correction get more weight in the blend (hover a factor for details). Rerun tools/calibrate_alta.py to refresh.
  3. Storm direction. Alta does best in northwest flow and the models know it least well. Each day is labelled by its 700 mb (about 10,000 ft) wind: NW, W, SW or other. Over the same two winters each model's misses were split by that label, and its water is scaled again for the day's direction. Directions with few wet days are pulled toward no change:
    ModelNWWSWOther
  4. Temperature. Each model's temperature is compared with the Atwater sensor in 3-hour blocks of the day. The miss swings with the season and with snow on the ground (winter nights run 4–6°F off for some models, fall nights 0–2°F), so the correction averages two measures: a table by calendar month from every month since March 2024, and a rolling bias from the last 14 days, re-measured each morning for each lead day. For this month, 1 day out:
    Model12–3a3–6a6–9a9a–12p12–3p3–6p6–9p9p–12a
    The corrected temperature also feeds the rain/snow split and the snow ratio. Atwater sits at 8,750 ft, so this assumes each model's miss is the same at 9,895 ft. The scorecard tracks whether it helps.
  5. Wind. No SNOTEL near Alta measures wind, so the nightly scorecard worker saves Hidden Peak's readings and learns the correction from them.
  6. Rain or snow. From the temperature at 9,895 ft: all snow at 32°F or colder, all rain at 37.4°F or warmer, a straight line between (Jennings et al. 2018).
  7. Snow ratio. Kuchera method: 12:1 at about 29°F, heavier above, lighter below, from the warmer of the 9,895 ft and 700 mb temperatures.
  8. Days 8–15. The corrections are only measured out to 7 days, and a single model run that far out is mostly noise, so from day 8 the snow and water are the average of all 82 ensemble runs rather than the four-model blend. The model table still shows each model's own run.
  9. Odds and ranges. The same steps run on all 82 runs of the ECMWF (51) and GFS (31) ensembles, each corrected with its parent model's factor (the GFS ensemble keeps one grid throughout, so its factor is held at the day-4 value). "Likely range" takes how spread out the middle half of those runs is and centres it on the blend; chance figures are the share of runs that hit the mark.
  10. Everything else (temperature, feels like, humidity, wind, cloud, freezing level) is the weighted blend of the four models. Snow level comes from the NWS forecast grid and runs out after about 7 days.

Sources: Open-Meteo forecast, ensemble and previous-runs APIs (NOAA, ECMWF, Environment Canada models) · NWS api.weather.gov (snow level, forecast snow, alerts, forecast discussion) · NRCS SNOTEL 1308 Atwater. Nothing comes from OpenSnow or the resort.
